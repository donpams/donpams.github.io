About me

Every aircraft carries functions it simply cannot get wrong. Right now, my job is making sure they don't. As an Air Dominance Systems Engineer at Boeing, I work in Systems Engineering, Integration & Test on the T-7A Red Hawk, tracing how safety-critical functions can fail across more than 25 system threads and closing airworthiness gaps on the aircraft.

I'm most useful where disciplines meet. I trained in electrical and computer engineering at USC, and my work so far has spanned embedded hardware and firmware, model-based systems engineering and software. Moving between them has taught me to follow a problem from a single signal up to the behavior of a whole system, and to explain it clearly to the people who need to act on it.

I don't treat any of that as a fixed lane. I'm happiest when I'm learning something new, and at the moment that's artificial intelligence: I'm working through an M.S. at Johns Hopkins and building projects like Safe Autopilot to see how learned systems behave when the stakes are real. Whatever the next interesting problem turns out to be, in AI, software, hardware or a field I haven't touched yet, I want to be the one picking it up.

Bayesian ML / Safety2026

Bird Strike Radar

Solo builder

An interactive map of damaging bird-strike risk at 266 US commercial airports, treated as a system safety problem. A Bayesian hierarchical model estimates monthly damage rates with honest uncertainty, and a Mission Planner turns them into the chance of a damaging strike for a planned set of operations, classified on the MIL-STD-882E risk matrix.

Details

Built on 242,449 public FAA strike reports from 2010 to 2025. The negative binomial model pools information across airports, flyways and seasons, so only differences the data actually supports stand out on the map. You can play through the year to watch the spring and autumn migration waves.

Fitted on 2010 to 2022 and scored on 2023 to 2025, it beat a simpler baseline by about five standard errors on log score. Public data only, not for operational use.
